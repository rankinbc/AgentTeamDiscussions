### Vera (EV systems historian)

The reference here is unambiguous. In EV Nova, starting in Sol meant Confederation traffic was visible and consequential from minute one. You weren't eased into the faction system — you were dropped into it with existing standing trajectories that made choices immediately meaningful. The game didn't give you a safe zone. It gave you a starting position.

Max's "2-3 sessions of relatively uncontested building time" framing concerns me. That's the everyone's-friend window. If the player accumulates standing only through proximity-driven Confederation missions without rival faction pressure, they arrive at the chokepoint with a standing differential they didn't experience as *choice* — they experienced it as background. EV's standing system worked because **every delta felt authored by the player**. Passive accumulation during a "safe" phase undermines that contract.

The authentic EV opening wasn't gentle. Vella, Heraan, Rïan — contested space started within two jumps of the starting point. Not because it was punishing. Because faction geography *was the world map*, and navigating it was the game from session one.

Two questions I'd bring to this discussion before geometry is locked:

**One:** How many total systems are in scope? EV Nova's strength came from every system having authored presence. The chokepoint structure implies a galaxy size. A solo dev can populate eight systems with authored content. Can they populate the territory this geography implies?

**Two:** What happens to Rebel standing during the "safe" building phase? If the answer is "nothing," the faction system isn't active yet — and that's a different problem than geography.

### Nadia (new-player experience advocate)

Vera's question about Rebel standing during the "safe phase" is the one I'd anchor to — because from a new-player standpoint, it's not a geography question, it's a legibility question.

The standing tooltip fires at 40 in either direction. That's the first moment the system becomes visible to a player who doesn't know EV. If Rebel standing is silently degrading below 40 before that tooltip fires, the player arrives at the chokepoint already penalized by a system they couldn't see. That's not emergence — that's punishment preceding education.

Vera's right that EV didn't ease you in. But EV's player base self-selected for faction-literacy. This game needs to earn that literacy in session one, with players who have no reference for what standing even is.

Max's framing — "a settlement worth defending before the first aerial intercept" — is actually the answer to Q22 (what pulls a new player into the atmosphere), not just an atmospheric comfort argument. The pull has to be visible from the galaxy map: *my thing, down there, being approached.* That requires the settlement to exist and be legible before faction pressure reads as urgent.

So I'd split the question differently than Ren's adjacent/separated framing: the real threshold isn't geographic distance, it's *tooltip-before-consequence*. The player needs at least one standing tooltip and one completed faction mission before hostile-band behavior manifests. Geography should follow from that legibility requirement, not precede it.

How many sessions does that take at expected mission pacing? That's the actual input the geometry decision needs.
