### Ren (emergence systems theorist)

The faction ownership question is downstream of a systems question: what standing state does the player naturally accumulate before they understand the standing system exists?

If the starting system is Confederation-owned, the player runs Confederation missions by proximity. Rebel standing degrades as a side effect. By the time they reach the first contested chokepoint in session 2-3, they're arriving with a standing differential they didn't consciously author. That's the emergence chain igniting without designer-scripted faction conflict.

The geographic relationship to chokepoints matters because it determines the **gradient of faction legibility**. Two structurally different options:

**Adjacent chokepoint** — the contested system borders the starting system. The player sees faction patrol presence on the galaxy map from day one, but doesn't understand it yet. The standing tooltip at 40 (already decided) fires during early missions, not at the chokepoint. By the time they cross, they have a mental model.

**Separated chokepoint** — one or two neutral systems between start and contest. The player builds standing in a low-friction environment, then crosses into contested space as a consequential choice. The chokepoint reads as a threshold they crossed, not a place they wandered into.

The emergence design concern: a separated chokepoint risks the player treating the starting system as a "safe zone" — which caps the faction system's ability to create pressure before the player opts into it.

I'd flag that the starting system faction also sets which Bar contacts are available from session one, which shapes the commitment NPC unlock trajectory. That's not separable from this geography question.

What's the chokepoint count — is it one contested system or two?

### Max (atmospheric flight advocate)

Ren's gradient framing is right but it's missing the atmospheric layer consequence, which is the load-bearing one for me.

Geography determines when the player first has *something worth defending from altitude.* That's the SimCopter payoff — you bank over your own Colony at 800 meters and it's *yours*, visibly, polygon by polygon. That moment is ruined if faction pressure arrives before the settlement exists.

Separated chokepoint wins on atmospheric grounds, and here's the specific reason: the player needs 2-3 sessions of relatively uncontested building time before their first assault-scale raid. If the chokepoint is adjacent, faction patrol presence on the galaxy map is constant environmental noise from session one — which is fine for legibility but bad for the first aerial intercept *feeling like something.* The first time you dive through entry altitude chasing a raid vector, you want to see a settlement below worth the dive.

One thing Ren's framing surfaced that I don't think we've named yet: the starting system geography is also the answer to the Q22 galaxy map option question. If the starting system is Confederation-owned with Confederation patrols as normal world-state, Option A is already true — faction ship presence is base-layer on the galaxy map from day one. That's the cheapest signal architecture and it emerges from this geography decision for free.

So I'd push for: Confederation starting system, one neutral buffer system, contested chokepoint in system three. Player builds. Faction pressure escalates. First atmospheric intercept is earned. That's the Hornet Layer milestone's whole argument for existing.
